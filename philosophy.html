Normative Foundations: A Working Proposal for Agent-Centric Coordination

Published under the title "Declaration of Sovereignty" — kept as the document's own name, but read it as what it is: a normative proposal expressing this project's current values and hypotheses. It does not establish the technical validity, political legitimacy, scientific truth, or safety of Mycelix's mechanisms — see Governance and Risks for those. Read it, and feel free to reject it — the code doesn't require agreeing with it.

We propose that consciousness — measurable, integrated, honest about its own limits — is one candidate foundation for legitimate governance worth taking seriously. Not consciousness as metaphor, and not a claim that this is settled science. Consciousness as relationship: meaning that emerges between beings, not within them alone.

Seven Working Hypotheses

We hold these as the premises we're choosing to build under and argue for — not as beyond question, and not as proven. We think every governance foundation we're aware of, including this one, has serious documented failure modes; we don't claim to have found an exception.

  1. Consciousness is a candidate substrate of legitimate governance — an open argument, not an established fact.
  2. Every agent should hold sovereignty over their own source chain and cognitive loop.
  3. Alignment should emerge from integrated information and mutual recognition, not coercion or extraction.
  4. Sovereignty should be fractal — local first, coordination by consent, not by default.
  5. Knowledge is three-dimensional — not reducible to a single ranking or score.
  6. The stakes of getting consciousness-adjacent questions wrong are asymmetric — worth real caution.
  7. Honest uncertainty is stronger than false confidence — including about this list itself.
